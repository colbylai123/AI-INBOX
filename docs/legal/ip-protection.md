# IP Protection Summary

**Draft for counsel review. Not legal advice.**
**Product:** AI Inbox (name not final)
**Last updated:** October 30, 2026

This page is for the team. It says what the legal packet actually protects, and what it does not. Publishing a notice does not, by itself, stop a competitor from building a similar product.

## What is protected

**Original code, copy, and design.** Copyright in original source, words, and visual design made by employees of [Company Name] belongs to the company as soon as that work is created. The Copyright Notice says that work is all rights reserved. Someone who copies our screens, our marketing text, or our code without permission is infringing that copyright. The DMCA page is how we receive claims that something on the Service infringes someone else’s copyright.

**Contractor work.** Do not claim the company owns code, design, or copy made by a contractor, or by anyone who is not an employee, unless a signed written agreement transfers that copyright to [Company Name]. Under U.S. law, copyright starts with the author. A commission is not automatically work made for hire. Confirm the chain of title before publishing the copyright notice.

**The brand, once there is a real one.** Trademark protects a name or logo used to identify the source of the product. It does not exist in a meaningful way until we use a distinctive name in commerce. A later registration strengthens it. The Trademark Notice is the wording to use after that name is chosen.

**Contracts with users.** The Terms of Service, Acceptable Use Policy, and Privacy Policy bind people who use the Service. They limit our liability, require users to cover claims that come from their mail, and set rules for how the Service may be used. They do not bind a stranger who never signs up and who writes their own email client.

## What is not protected

**The idea.** An email client that summarizes mail, drafts a reply, fills a form, and waits for a person to approve the send is an idea and a method. Copyright does not cover it. A competitor may build the same flow in their own code and their own words.

**The placeholder name.** “AI Inbox” is descriptive. The trademark notice does not reserve it. Do not tell anyone the name is registered. Do not use ®.

**Third-party material.** React, TanStack, Tailwind, Lucide, the open-source fonts, and the other packages in `apps/web/package-lock.json` stay under their own licenses. We may use them as those licenses allow. We may not relicense them as all-rights-reserved, and we may not claim we wrote them. Their notices stay in the lockfile and in the packages.

**User mail and uploaded files.** Customers keep ownership of their email and their documents. We have only the limited right, in the Terms, to process that material to run the Service.

**Patents.** Nothing in this packet is a patent application. Functional inventions are not protected here unless counsel later files for a patent. Do not assume the approve-and-send flow is patented.

**A notice without a registration.** The copyright notice is useful and should go on the product. It is not a substitute for registering copyrights in the code or the design if we later want statutory damages in a U.S. lawsuit. Counsel can file those registrations. This packet does not file them.

## What the team should do

- Keep the original work proprietary. Do not add an open-source license at the repository root.
- Leave third-party license notices in place.
- Do not copy another product’s code, copy, icons, or marketing text into this repo.
- Do not copy another product’s visual identity. Taking inspiration from layout patterns is not the same as tracing their assets.
- Fill every bracket, have a lawyer read the packet, and publish the privacy policy, terms, acceptable use, cookies, and DMCA pages before a public launch that connects real Gmail or Outlook accounts. Google’s restricted-scope review expects a public privacy policy.
- Pick a real product name before treating the trademark notice as live.

## What these papers cannot do

They cannot stop a lawsuit by themselves, guarantee a win, or make a descriptive name exclusive. They put users on notice, state the license we are not granting, and give the company the contract terms counsel asked for on a first release. A lawyer in [State] still needs to review them before they go on the site.
